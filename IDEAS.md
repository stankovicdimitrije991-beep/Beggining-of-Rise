# Idea scorecard

Scores are 1–5 (5 = best). Constraint: 2–3 hours a day next to a full-time job.

| # | Idea | Speed to first $ | Income potential | Fits your skills | Competition (5 = low) | Cost to start (5 = cheap) | Total |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | AI-assisted dispatch service | 5 | 4 | 5 | 3 | 5 | **22** |
| 4 | AI driver recruiting for carriers | 4 | 5 | 4 | 3 | 3 | **19** |
| 5 | Finding clients for a video editor friend | 4 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 5 | **17** |
| 3 | Faceless trucking page | 2 | 3 | 4 | 2 | 5 | **16** |
| 2 | AI software for carriers and dispatchers | 1 | 5 | 4 | 2 | 4 | **16** |

## 1. AI-assisted dispatch service: **start now**
- "AI dispatch" should mean **you plus AI**, not AI alone. Brokers still negotiate with people, and a fully automated dispatcher loses loads and trust.
- With AI handling emails, rate confirmations, invoices and check calls, one person can manage about 10–15 trucks instead of 5–8. That's your edge, and good marketing ("AI-powered dispatch, faster replies, weekly reports").
- Income: 5–10% of gross, about $1,000–1,750 per truck per month.

## 4. AI driver recruiting: **strongest second idea**
- Carriers have constant driver turnover and pay **about $500–2,500 per hired CDL driver**, or $50–150 per qualified lead.
- How it works: Facebook/Instagram/TikTok ads or organic posts → lead form → an **AI SMS/WhatsApp screener** asks the qualifying questions (CDL class, years of experience, location, home-time needs, violations) → qualified drivers are sent to the carrier with a summary.
- Start with one carrier, ideally one you dispatch for. Let **the carrier pay the ad budget** so you don't risk your own money.
- Compliance: the carrier runs MVRs, background checks and drug tests. You never pull those reports yourself. Follow SMS consent rules (TCPA): drivers must opt in.
- The AI screener is your **first software product** (idea #2) built for real use.

## 5. Clients for your video editor friend: **merge with #3 and #4**
- Referral fees (10–20%) are small money. The bigger version is an agency: you find clients, your friend edits, and you keep 30–50%. But income is limited by how many hours your friend can edit.
- Better: **carriers and dispatch companies are your clients too**. They need recruiting videos (for #4) and social content. Sell "video content for trucking companies", edited by your friend.
- Have your friend edit your own trucking page (#3) in exchange for the leads you bring them.

## 3. Faceless trucking page: **funnel, not a main income**
- The trucking niche pays well for ads and affiliates (insurance, factoring, CDL schools, ELDs).
- Risk: YouTube cuts monetization on mass-produced, repetitive AI content, and pure AI faceless pages are very crowded. Use **real footage, real load numbers and real stories**. Your voice and screen recordings can still keep it faceless.
- Use it to find drivers (#4), carriers (#1) and future software users (#2).

## 2. AI software: **build from what you already use, don't start from zero**
- Build tools you need for #1 and #4 first (the driver screener, a rate con reader, a dispatch workspace).
- Sell them later to **Balkan dispatch companies**: one deal covers several dispatchers, for $200–1,000/month.

## Order of operations

| When | Focus |
|---|---|
| Month 1–3 | #1: sign the first 2–3 trucks |
| Month 2–4 | #4: pilot with one carrier; build the AI screener with Claude Code |
| Month 2+ | #3 + #5: your friend edits, you post 30 minutes a day; sell recruiting videos to carriers |
| Month 6+ | #2: package the internal tools and sell to dispatch companies |

**Rule:** with 2–3 hours a day, have at most **two active projects** at once. The rest only feed into those two.
